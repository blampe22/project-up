The Story · November 22, 2026

The Day Preconstruction Got Out of Hand

It started with a nursery mural for Auggie and Theo. Divide the artwork into forty panels, let the family paint them, and put the pieces together into one big adventure. It was a lovely, simple idea right up until Ben decided to run it like a construction job.

In the running joke, Katie played the demanding Owner. In real life she was supportive from the start. Ben volunteered for the paperwork all on his own. Soon a family painting project had a Project Manual, a Gantt schedule, a stack of addenda, and a website.

Then came the first meaningful inspection of the website. Eleven addenda had already gone out. Addendum No. 012 documented the Architect’s premature confidence and prompted a round of quality-control corrections. Somewhere in that review, the team realized a simple email could do the job we’d been designing an entire registration database to handle.

Ben took to delegation with enthusiasm. Theostus and Codex earned formal recognition as project consultants, and somewhere between handing off assignments and checking what came back, Ben realized he’d learned how to build a website.

  1. Ben

    Dude, I just learned how to build a website.

  2. Theostus

    You didn't just learn how to build a website. You learned that building one is now within your reach.

As of November 22, the Owner hadn’t even seen the website. Preconstruction, on the other hand, had thoroughly enjoyed itself.

Project UP is where imagination meets probability, and even the fun has a contingency allowance. Under all the paperwork was the reason the family started: making something together as a family that Auggie and Theo can enjoy for years.

Corrective Action: None recommended. The results have been entertaining.

Document Control Finding: At no point did anyone suggest renaming the conversation. This is now considered a document-control failure.

The Owner may wish to review the Architect's definition of “quick idea.”

From the Project Trailer

Owner Review No. 001 — The Executive Dashboard Directive

After an extensive preconstruction effort involving twelve addenda, a comprehensive Project Manual, a master schedule, and an interactive website, Project UP was finally presented to its Owner.

Katie reviewed the proposed mural.

Her first comment was immediate.

Katie: “40 panels?!”

Ben, who had somehow made it this far without fully appreciating the implications of manufacturing forty individual pieces, offered a reassuring response.

Ben: “Uhh... yeah, I didn’t really think about it.”

Katie had raised a valid concern.

At this point, Ben had yet to print a single mural panel. In fact, he hadn't entirely figured out how he was going to print them.

There were still some minor technical questions surrounding fabrication, assembly, and whether the whole operation was remotely practical.

But those were problems for the construction phase.

For now, Ben had more pressing priorities.

He needed a polished website, an interactive mural selection system, and a compelling family participation experience.

After all, how was he supposed to convince forty people to paint pieces of a mural if he didn't have a website to sell them the space?

Editor's Note: The project had successfully advanced from an unproven fabrication concept to a fully developed marketing operation.

Current Project Status:

  • Website development: Progressing nicely.

Following additional evaluation, the Owner identified opportunities to improve readability and project progress visibility.

Fabrication Readiness Review

The team converted the approved artwork into a working master, prepared one eight-inch paintability sample with flush black outlines on white PLA, and sliced it for the Bambu P1S. The sample was ready for a test print. Physical production remained at zero.

During earlier planning, Ben and Theostus had already compared the paperwork with the output from the fabrication department.

  1. Ben

    All this time in planning and I made no actual progress on the project we're doing than getting the proof of concept and telling a funny story.

  2. Theostus

    Your physical percent complete? Approximately 0%. Your administrative percent complete? 147%.

The preliminary schedule listed December 5, 2026 as a planning date. Management had yet to approve a fabrication start date.

The Fabrication Department’s Deferred Maintenance Program

With the slicer file ready, the fabrication department prepared to move from preconstruction to an actual test print. The printer had other plans.

During preparation, the team discovered that the project’s sole printer had been having trouble feeding filament. The Owner acknowledged she already knew about the issue, which helped explain the lack of activity in the fabrication reports.

The team traced a broken piece of filament through the printhead tubing and rear buffer, eventually isolating the obstruction in the AMS. Several attempts to extract it were unsuccessful. The obstruction remains and repair is ongoing.

Management classified the incident as equipment maintenance. The absence of an approved fabrication start date prevented a schedule delay from being established.

Meanwhile, the Owner reviewed the newly commissioned website and requested clearer navigation, a better-organized project story, and simpler ways for family members to participate. The website team promptly received another assignment.

Current Project Status

  • Website operational, with refinements underway.
  • First prototype designed and ready for printing.
  • AMS filament obstruction identified. Repair ongoing.
  • Physical panels completed: zero.
  • Official schedule delay: not established.

The project team remains confident that physical fabrication will begin shortly after the equipment becomes operational, provided no additional website improvements are identified.
