Behind the Adventure

Forty Panels. Twelve Addenda. Zero Panels Fabricated.
And somehow, an entire website.

The Story · November 22, 2026

The Day Preconstruction Got Out of Hand

Ben wanted the family to paint a nursery mural for Auggie and Theo. Then he decided to run it like a construction job. Katie was supportive. Ben volunteered for the paperwork.

Eleven addenda went out before Ben properly inspected the website. Addendum 012 documented the correction. The team also discovered that one email could replace a registration database.

By the time Ben enlisted Theostus and Codex, he’d learned how to build a website. Katie still hadn’t seen it.

  1. Ben

    Dude, I just learned how to build a website.

  2. Theostus

    You didn't just learn how to build a website. You learned that building one is now within your reach.

Under all the paperwork was a simple idea. Make something together that the boys could enjoy for years.

Katie saw the mural and immediately spotted the constructability issue.

Ben still had to figure out fabrication. But how was he supposed to convince forty people to paint a mural without a website to sell them the space?

Katie asked for clearer navigation and progress visibility. The AI team interpreted that as an Executive Reporting Dashboard. Ben had spent his working hours building those in Power BI.

Ben types his emoji directions. Translation has been delegated.

Fabrication Readiness Review

The team prepared the working mural artwork and sliced one eight-inch black-and-white paintability sample for the Bambu P1S. Physical panels completed remained at zero.

  1. Ben

    All this time in planning and I made no actual progress on the project we're doing than getting the proof of concept and telling a funny story.

  2. Theostus

    Your physical percent complete? Approximately 0%. Your administrative percent complete? 147%.

December 5, 2026 appeared on the preliminary schedule. Management had yet to approve a fabrication start date.

The Fabrication Department’s Deferred Maintenance Program

With the file ready, the printer refused to feed filament. Katie acknowledged she’d known about the issue. The team traced the broken filament through the printhead tubing and rear buffer to the AMS, where it remains.

Management classified the incident as maintenance. With no approved fabrication start date, no schedule delay could be established.

Meanwhile, Katie reviewed the website. The development team promptly received another assignment.
