Behind the Adventure

Forty Panels. Twelve Addenda. Zero Panels Fabricated.
And somehow, an entire website.

The Story · November 22, 2026

The Day Preconstruction Got Out of Hand

It started with a nursery mural for Auggie and Theo. Divide the artwork into forty panels, let the family paint them, and put the pieces together into one big adventure. It was a lovely, simple idea right up until the Project Architect decided to run it like a construction job.

In the running joke, Katie played the demanding Owner. In real life she was supportive from the start. The Architect volunteered for the paperwork all on his own. Soon a family painting project had a Project Manual, a Gantt schedule, a stack of addenda, and a website.

Then came the first meaningful inspection of the website. Eleven addenda had already gone out. Addendum No. 012 documented the Architect’s premature confidence and prompted a round of quality-control corrections. Somewhere in that review, we realized a simple email could do the job we’d been designing an entire registration database to handle.

The Architect took to delegation with enthusiasm. Theostus and Codex earned formal recognition as project consultants, and somewhere between handing off assignments and checking what came back, the Architect realized he’d learned how to build a website.

When he finally looked up fabrication on his own Gantt schedule, the start date was December 5. As of November 22, the Owner hadn’t even seen the website. Preconstruction, on the other hand, had thoroughly enjoyed itself.

Project UP is where imagination meets probability, and even the fun has a contingency allowance. Under all the paperwork was the reason we started: making something together as a family that Auggie and Theo can enjoy for years.

Exhibit A — The Original Scope

Chat Thread Title: Nursery Crib Wall Idea

Original Intent: Discuss an idea for decorating the nursery crib wall.

Actual Outcome: A 40-panel family mural, twelve formal addenda, a Project Manual, a Gantt chart, an interactive website, and an AI-powered construction management department.

Scope Variance: Significant.

Root Cause: The Project Architect was left unsupervised with access to artificial intelligence.

Corrective Action: None recommended. The results have been entertaining.

Document Control Finding: At no point did anyone suggest renaming the conversation. This is now considered a document-control failure.

The Owner may wish to review the Architect's definition of “quick idea.”
